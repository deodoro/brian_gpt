2. Suppose all agents have lexicographic preferences for either  X or Y.  At least 
one agent has lexicographic preferences for X over Y, and at least one agent 
has lexicographic preferences for Y over X.   All agents start with X>0 and Y>0.  
 
True, False, and Explain:   A unique general equilibrium exist s. 
 
TRUE.  As long as 0<(p X/pY)<∞, everyone with a lexicographic preference for X  will want to sell 
ALL  his Y, and everyone with a lexicographic  preference for Y will want to sell ALL  his X.  But to 
clear the market, the market value of all the X on the market must equal the market value of all 
the Y on the market.  This happens when p X*(total  X endowments of Y -preferrers)=p Y*(total  Y 
endowments of X -preferrers); in other words, when  
 
pX/pY=(total Y endowments of X -preferrers)/(total X endowments of Y -preferrers), the unique 
general equilibrium.  For example, if the X -preferrers start with a total of 200 Y, and the Y -
preferrers start with a total of 100 X, the unique GE is p X/pY=200/100=2.
