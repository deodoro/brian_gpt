3. "The economically correct argument is the most politically incorrect argument imaginable." (Landsburg, The Armchair Economist)

True, False, and Explain: Landsburg is explaining that laws restricting women's use of cosmetic surgery make women in general worse off.

False. *Pg. 220-222
Women are not necessarily made worse off by laws restricting women's use of cosmetic surgery--specifically breast implants. Landsburg says that what matters may not be overall breast size, but relative breast size, so a ban on breast implants may not make women worse off in the dating market necessarily. If regulations were such that only one woman could get breast implants, she would be better off in the dating market, and all other women slightly disadvantaged, but not terribly. Instead, a ban on breast implants would make society overall worse off. Because men and the casual observer derive benefits from women getting breast implants, a ban on breast implant would reduce the overall benefits to society.

4[3]. A group of N oligopolists producing identical products plays a finite number of turns. Two of the players are then randomly selected to play a Hawk-Dove game ONCE.
 
True, False, and Explain: With rational, selfish players, some degree of collusion may be sustainable under Cournot competition, but NOT Bertrand competition.

True. Cournot case can have tacit collusion even in a one shot game, but in Bertrand case you either have explicit collusion or the perfectly competitive outcome. Working backwards, the outcome of Hawk-Dove isn't going to affect the outcome.
