9. A critic of behavioral economics runs the following experiment. Half of the subjects guess the populations of twenty countries. The remaining subjects try to list as many people as they can from each of the twenty countries. The true populations of these countries are known to the experimenter.
 
True, False, and Explain: This data can be used to test for availability bias.

False? A better test for availability bias would have the subjects guess the populations of 
countries they are most knowledgeable about. The participant should have a high correlation between their knowledge of the country and the value of the estimated population.[15]
This test asks about number of people who can be named, but a key thing for the availability heuristic is ease and fluency of recall. If people can name lots of people but find it very difficult, availability bias might make them less likely to see the population as large, rather than more. Number of names and ease of naming must be correlated for this to be an effective test.
