3.) Paul Krugman recently accused anti-Keynesians of being "knaves and fools."  What would Kahneman say about this accusation?  Use Thinking, Fast and Slow  to craft a thoughtful reply.


The halo effect may play a role here, with Krugman reaching the conclusion that people are "knaves and fools" because they hold an opinion on economic science that he believes is mistaken. The halo effect occurs when people develop a unified view of a person or entity to create a coherent narrative. So one negative thing about someone (such as a mistaken opinion on economics) will support a broader narrative of them as a fool or a knave. 

Krugman may also be suffering from an availability bias--it could be that Krugman can easily name many anti-Keynesians who have acted in what he perceives to be a foolish or knavish manner, and this bias causes him to evaluate anti-Keynesians as knaves or fools. It is true that experts are less likely to to be affected by ease of retrieval, but those who are powerful (which Krugman most certainly is in the intellectual landscape) are MORE likely to do this.

Also confirmation bias. Krugman is more likely to seek out information that confirms his pre-existing beliefs, preventing him from updating properly.
