2.) Use the signaling model of education to explain the sheepskin effect. Precisely specify why, in the real world, discrete educational achievement is so much more informative to employers than continuous educational achievement.

The sheepskin effect describes the fact that the vast majority of the returns to education are achieved upon completing the final year and graduating. This is consistent with the signaling model, which says that education is mostly about showing that you can make it through school and incur costs that others cannot, revealing to potential employers what "type" you are. 

In the real world, discrete educational achievement is more important than continuous educational achievement because schools do not impart many skills to you--the human capital effects of education are quite small. We can see this happening in the real world--if skills really mattered, I could theoretically move to New Jersey, attend classes at Princeton (the greatest university the world has ever known) without matriculating, and gain the skills from those courses to increase my salary. However, employers don't care if you've attended the courses, only if you have obtained the degree. Discrete educational achievement is more informative because it sends the signal (the diploma) that you can bear the full costs of your schooling, providing employers with more information about your "type" and enhancing your employment prospects.
