1. In a world with no externalities, suppose the government imposes a lump sum tax and uses it to fund an hourly work subsidy. 
 
True, False, and Explain:  The deadweight cost of this tax-and-subsidy policy is zero.

False. It is true that the DWL of a lump sum tax is zero. However, assuming the market starts out in equilibrium, a subsidy carries a DWL because it distorts the allocation of resources.  There would be a loss in both consumer and producer surplus.  Supply Shifts out beyond the natural equilibrium level because you are trying to increase the labor supply. 

Caplan seemed to like this answer. He said that this would cause labor to be allocated to lower valued uses. And a lump sum tax to pay for wage subsidies to deal with efficiency wage unemployment doesn't make sense because you'd be taxing the unemployed and then subsidizing them to do a lower-valued job.
